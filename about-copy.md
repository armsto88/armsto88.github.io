# About

## Turning ecological questions into monitoring systems
I work across field ecology, software, electronics and environmental sensing to create monitoring systems that are reliable in the field and easier to understand.

### Background

#### From sound systems to ecological systems

My path into conservation technology began in audio engineering before moving through field conservation and zoology into ecological monitoring design.

I trained in Audio Engineering and worked in live sound, location recording and post-production. This gave me a practical understanding of microphones, recording systems and signal chains that now supports my work in passive acoustic monitoring and bioacoustics.

I later worked in bush regeneration and conservation fieldwork on the Sunshine Coast, Australia. My experience included habitat restoration, species identification, bird surveys, Glossy Black-Cockatoo monitoring and acoustic recorder deployment.

While completing a Bachelor of Science in Zoology, I became increasingly interested in the tools behind ecological monitoring and how hardware, software and field methods can work together.

What connects my work is an interest in the whole monitoring process: defining the ecological question, understanding field constraints, selecting or building the right tools, managing the data and presenting the results clearly.

### Approach

#### How I approach a monitoring problem

I start with the ecological question. From there, I choose the hardware, software and level of automation around the evidence needed and the realities of field deployment.

1. **Define the ecological question** — Identify what needs to be observed, which decision the evidence should support and what level of detail is genuinely useful.
2. **Design for the field** — Account for power, weather, communications, servicing intervals and the people responsible for deploying and maintaining the system.
3. **Close the data loop** — Plan data transfer, storage, validation, visualisation and analysis alongside the hardware so observations become useful ecological information.

---

### Working principles

#### Practical, legible and maintainable

- Ecological usefulness matters more than technical novelty.
- Field testing should begin early enough to influence the design.
- Local data storage and clear failure states are essential in remote deployments.
- Interfaces are part of the monitoring method, not an afterthought.
- Automation should remain reviewable and transparent about uncertainty.
- Clear systems are easier for teams to operate, diagnose and improve.
